Project 02 — Behind the Scenes

A deep dive into the process behind the improvements to Immanuelskyrkan’s website

Step 01

Community Research

To understand Immanuel Church’s digital landscape, I began by meeting with key stakeholders to capture their perspectives, priorities, and concerns. These conversations revealed not only what they hoped the website could achieve, but also the pain points holding it back. It gave me a clear sense of the internal expectations and the strategic direction they wanted to move toward.

I then dug into their existing analytics to map out user behavior, traffic patterns, and the pages that carry the most weight. This data exposed where users naturally gravitate, where they drop off, and which parts of the site demand the most attention. It grounded the project in evidence rather than assumptions.

To complete the picture, I conducted interviews with real users: seniors, youth, and Korean‑speaking members. Their experiences, frustrations, and mental models revealed gaps that neither stakeholders nor analytics could fully capture. This combination of qualitative and quantitative insights set a strong foundation for every design decision that followed.

Deliverable Research findings, design principles
Step 02

Ideation & Design Direction

With the research insights established, the next phase focused on turning observations into actionable design opportunities. The concepts were developed within the organization’s existing framework and resources, ensuring the solutions stayed both realistic and scalable.

I explored multiple directions through rapid sketching, both on paper and in Figma. This allowed me to test different navigation structures, content hierarchies, and visual approaches without getting stuck in details too early. The goal was to uncover solutions that feel intuitive the moment a user sees them.

As the concepts evolved, menus, imagery, and layout patterns began to form a cohesive system. The ideation phase wasn’t just about generating ideas, it was about shaping a direction that balances usability, aesthetics, and technical reality.

Deliverable Design directions
Step 03

Redesign and Testing

Once the strongest concepts emerged, I transformed the sketches into low‑fidelity prototypes to validate structure and flow. These prototypes acted as a fast, flexible testing ground where ideas could be challenged, refined, or discarded before moving into high‑fidelity design.

The navigation became the core focus of the redesign. It needed to be clearer, more intuitive, and more representative of the church’s diverse audience. A dedicated section for seniors was introduced as a new navigation attribute, giving them a direct and accessible entry point. This change was tested with users to ensure it felt logical, discoverable, and genuinely helpful.

I then developed high‑fidelity prototypes in Figma, complete with interactive components and simulated hover states to mirror real behavior. These prototypes allowed users to experience the redesign as it would function in practice, making the testing phase more accurate and the feedback more actionable.

Deliverable Low-Fidelity web concept prototype, Offline touchpoint concepts
Step 04

Final Result

The final outcome was a polished high‑fidelity prototype that significantly reduces user frustration and shortens the path to key goals, as confirmed through user testing. The design is not only visually aligned with the church’s identity but also structurally optimized for the way their community actually navigates the site.

The prototype is fully prepared for implementation within WordPress, respecting both technical constraints and editorial workflows. It gives the web team a clear, actionable blueprint for bringing the redesign to life without guesswork.

This final delivery represents a user‑centered, data‑driven, and future‑ready direction for Immanuel Church’s digital presence, one that supports visitors, empowers members, and strengthens the church’s ability to communicate with clarity and confidence.
